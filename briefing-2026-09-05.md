## 🤖 AI

### Claude Formalizes First Complete Proof of Fermat's Last Theorem
Anthropic revealed that dozens of Claude agents, running on the Prove2Me platform paired with a Claude Code-based multi-agent workflow, produced the first fully machine-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in the Lean 4 proof assistant — in just 11 days. The formalization runs to over 13 million lines of Lean code and 29,500 new theorems, roughly five times the size of Lean's entire existing math library, and Anthropic says it uses only Lean's three standard axioms with no placeholder or omitted steps. Mathematician Kevin Buzzard called it an extraordinary achievement, arriving far faster than experts had predicted and pointing toward automated formalization of the broader mathematical literature.

### OpenAI Launches GPT-6 Astra, Declares the "AGI Era"
OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra on September 3, with president Greg Brockman telling a closed press briefing "welcome to the AGI era." Trained on OpenAI's largest-ever run using more than 100,000 GPUs at its Texas Stargate site, Astra sets new benchmarks in computer use, browsing, coding, science and professional work, and is designed to operate software — browsers, spreadsheets, desktop apps — much like a person would. It's also the first OpenAI model to trip the company's "critical" cybersecurity threshold, meaning it can potentially find and exploit unknown vulnerabilities without step-by-step human guidance; cyber capabilities are being gated as it rolls out to ChatGPT Plus/Pro/Business/Enterprise and API users "in the coming days," priced at $10/$50 per million tokens with a 1M-token context window.

## 💻 Technology

### Feds Open Safety Probe Into Tesla's Cybercab Hours After Austin Launch
NHTSA opened an investigation into Tesla's Cybercab — which has no steering wheel or pedals — mere hours after Tesla began offering rides on Austin streets. Tesla self-certified the vehicle as compliant with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards, but those standards currently require equipment like brake pedals and rearview mirrors that the Cybercab lacks; the Department of Transportation has proposed loosening those rules for autonomous-only vehicles, but the change isn't final. NHTSA says it will scrutinize how much of Tesla's certification rested on the assumption that certain standards simply don't apply.
