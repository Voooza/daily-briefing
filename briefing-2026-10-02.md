## 🤖 AI

### Anthropic Ships "Mods" — Claude Code Becomes a Platform You Write Against
The most substantive overnight AI release wasn't a model. Anthropic pushed a broad Claude Code update whose headline feature is **Mods**: small TypeScript functions that change how Claude Code itself behaves. A mod can rewrite a prompt before it's sent, add new UI, replace a built-in feature outright, or bolt on functionality that didn't exist. Mods ship inside plugins, which makes them installable and shareable rather than a private hack.

Alongside it: a **beta redesign of Claude Code projects**, where a single project coordinates parallel threads, shared memory, and a project library — aimed squarely at long-running work that spans multiple repos and tasks rather than one-session-one-question usage.

The release also carried a long tail of platform work: stronger plugin and MCP behaviour, better model and session handling, accessibility and Windows fixes, plus improvements to cloud sessions, Remote Control, Bedrock, Vertex, and the VS Code integration.

Why it matters: every lab is converging on "the agent is the product," but they're taking different routes. OpenAI's answer this week was Dots — hosted, always-on agents you configure. Anthropic's is a coding agent you reprogram. One is an appliance, the other is a toolkit, and developers tend to reward toolkits.

Separately, Anthropic's **content watermarking rollout on Amazon Bedrock** is scheduled to complete by **October 12**.

### MIT's Stratego Player Beats the Humans — Efficiently
MIT researchers unveiled a game-playing AI that is now the **top-ranked Stratego player**, beating the best humans while being markedly more compute-efficient than comparable systems. Stratego is a deliberately awkward target: imperfect information, a huge branching factor, and bluffing as a core mechanic rather than an edge case. The researchers pitch the transferable version of the result at decision-making under hidden information — military manoeuvre planning and negotiation are the examples given.

The efficiency claim is the part worth filing away. "We beat humans" is 2016 news. "We beat humans for a fraction of the compute" is the thing that actually ships into products.
(source: [MIT News — Artificial Intelligence](https://news.mit.edu/topic/artificial-intelligence2))

### Gemini 4 Argon: The Numbers Landed, the Model Didn't
Detail filled in overnight on Google's frontier release. **Pricing: $2 per million input tokens, $10 per million output**, with cached input discounted **95%** — undercutting the usual frontier premium and matching GPT-6.1 Sol's headline rate almost exactly. Benchmarks Google is claiming: **77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1** (long-horizon software engineering) and **91.7% on LVBench** (long video understanding). The output token ceiling went from 64,000 to **one million**, which is the real architectural flex — room to reason at length in a single pass.

But availability is the story. Argon is going out **first to a limited set of trusted cyber defenders through Google's Fairwind programme**, with Google participating in the US government's voluntary pre-release access process. **No public release date was given** — just "as soon as possible," starting with paid API customers and AI Ultra subscribers.

Google's internal examples explain the caution: quantum researchers used Argon to optimise spacetime resources and beat a published baseline by **40% within minutes**, and on the security side Google says it can autonomously find, validate, and patch critical vulnerabilities. A model that good at finding exploitable bugs is a model you hand to defenders before you hand it to everyone.

## 🇨🇿 Czech News

### It's Over: The Government Survives, 83–94
After three days of debate spread across three sittings — the vote slipped twice because the chamber kept running out of day — the Chamber of Deputies finally voted. **The motion failed badly: 83 deputies backed it, 94 voted against, and 101 were needed.** Andrej Babiš's ANO–SPD–Motoristé coalition is intact.

Note the arithmetic. The opposition didn't just fall short of 101, it fell short of its own nominal strength — a 92-ish bloc produced 83 votes. Not a single coalition deputy defected. This was the opposition's **second failed attempt** to bring down the cabinet since it took office this year.

The charges were the ones they've been making all week: a widening state budget deficit and national debt, the **2027 draft budget's CZK 386 billion shortfall**, Babiš's unresolved conflict of interest as a billionaire prime minister, and unfulfilled campaign promises. Babiš called the whole exercise "one giant pre-election rally" and he was, on the substance, right — **municipal and Senate elections are on October 9–10**, one week out. The motion was never a mechanism for removal. It was a device for putting 200 deputies on the record eight days before voters go to the polls.
(sources: [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/10/01/czech-government-babis-confidence-vote/45657796-bdc8-11f1-81fc-9b76f8343b6c_story.html), [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/europe/20261002/e61585c4c4544248842888230ae2f9a1/c.html), [Expats.cz](https://www.expats.cz/czech-news/article/czech-government-under-pm-babis-survives-second-no-confidence-vote), [MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/czech-pm-babis-government-wins-confidence-vote-as-policies-on-budget-ukraine-shift-ce7e58ded88ef720), [Bluewin](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/czech-government-survives-no-confidence-vote-li.3621179))

### Defence Ministry Kills the CZK 24.7 Billion Armoured Vehicle Tender
The Ministry of Defence **cancelled its tender for up to 185 armoured vehicles**, estimated at **CZK 24.7 billion**, citing economic factors and "other circumstances" preventing the procurement from continuing. Ministry spokesperson **Petr Pešek** confirmed to Aktuálně.cz that bidders had been notified in line with public procurement law.

These weren't frontline combat vehicles. The 4×4 medium-weight platforms were specified in multiple configurations — ambulances, engineering and bomb-disposal vehicles, reconnaissance — intended for military engineers, medics, special forces, and the Military Police. The purchase was announced by **Petr Fiala's government in June 2025** and listed among the army's strategic modernisation projects.

**No timeline or details were given for a replacement tender.** The timing is pointed: this lands the same week the government survived a confidence vote fought partly over its budget, and in a 2027 budget where defence is one of the lines going *down*.

## 🏀 NBA

### Detroit Caved: Duren Gets Five Years, $200 Million, No Strings
The standoff ended on the deadline. With **Thursday the last day Jalen Duren could have signed his one-year, $9.6 million qualifying offer**, the Pistons and Duren agreed to a **five-year, $200 million rookie-scale extension**.

What broke the deadlock is the interesting part: **Detroit removed the conditioning clauses from the $200 million proposal entirely.** It was never really about the headline number — Duren had been genuinely prepared to take the $9.6 million and bet on himself rather than sign a deal with conditioning language attached. He skipped media day and the start of camp to make the point.

The escalation tells you who held the leverage: Detroit's offer went **$175M → $190M → $200M** over the past month, and then the team gave up the clauses too. A 22-year-old centre turned down $200 million guaranteed and got a *better* $200 million for it. File that one under negotiating tactics that actually worked.
