## 🤖 AI

### Nvidia Confirms $12.93 Billion Acquisition of Hugging Face
Nvidia officially confirmed it will buy Hugging Face for $12.93 billion — about $11.9 billion for investors plus up to $1 billion in employee retention — giving Jensen Huang's company control of the platform hosting over 3 million AI models, 1 million applications and half a million datasets used by 18+ million developers. Huang said Hugging Face will stay an open, multi-cloud, multi-accelerator platform, continuing to support hardware from AMD and Intel. Hugging Face reportedly approached Huang about the deal weeks in advance; it's Nvidia's second-biggest acquisition ever after the ~$20 billion Groq asset purchase, ahead of its 2019 Mellanox buy.

### Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max Pushes Open-Weight Frontier
Alibaba upgraded its Qwen line with a 2.4-trillion-parameter model, Qwen3.8-Max-0902, offering a 1-million-token context window at $2 per million tokens. It scored 93.0 on PaperBench and 86.1 on OSWorld-Verified, keeping pressure on OpenAI, Google and Anthropic in the open-weight race just a day after Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash launch and OpenAI's "Critical"-tier Astra disclosure.

## 🇨🇿 Czech News

### Government Confirms 2027 Budget Deficit of 389 Billion Kč, Raises Public-Sector Pay
Finance Minister Alena Schillerová (ANO) confirmed the cabinet's 2027 budget draft carries a 389-billion-Kč deficit — the second-highest in Czech history after 2021's pandemic-era 419.7-billion shortfall. Schillerová argues the increase is unavoidable, warning that holding the line would cause stagnation and higher costs later; the opposition calls it reckless given current growth. In the same session the government approved new public-sector pay tables, with the lowest wage tariff tied to the minimum wage, which rises from 22,400 to 24,900 Kč next year — a combined 36-billion-Kč commitment.

### Appellate Ruling Clears SPD Over Controversial Campaign Posters
As reported Thursday, the Prague Municipal Court overturned a three-million-crown fine against the SPD movement, ruling its 2024 election posters — including one invoking a knife-wielding migrant and another linking Roma children to smoking and truancy — were provocative but fell short of incitement to hatred. A separate immunity case against SPD leader and Chamber of Deputies speaker Tomio Okamura remains stalled.
