### Broadcom Starts Raising $60bn to Pay for Anthropic's Chips
Bloomberg reported Friday that Broadcom's bank syndicate has begun assembling a **$60 billion** financing package whose primary beneficiary is Anthropic. The structure: a **$42bn Class A senior-secured tranche** syndicated by Bank of America, Citigroup and Morgan Stanley, plus an **$18bn Class B junior tranche** led by Blackstone — which is committing **$9bn of its own capital** and syndicating the rest.

This is the funding behind the arrangement Anthropic's own IPO prospectus discloses: **Broadcom lending Anthropic up to $42bn in convertible notes** to finance chip leases, roughly a third of Anthropic's **$125.2bn five-year TPU capacity commitment**. The prospectus explicitly flags "potential conflicts of interest," and it is easy to see why — Broadcom is simultaneously Anthropic's chip supplier, its equipment lessor, and its lender.

Nothing is formally announced and all sourcing is unnamed, so treat the numbers as directional. But the shape is the story: this is the same move Nvidia made with its asset-manager structure in August — chips turned into collateral for off-balance-sheet lending. Compute is being financialised, and it is happening weeks before Anthropic faces public markets with roughly **$8bn of operating loss** on **$4.6bn of revenue** in its leaked prospectus.

### A Bad 48 Hours for AI Safety Teams
Three things happened in close succession, and together they read as one story.

**OpenAI fired three safety researchers** — Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni — after an internal review found they mishandled confidential information outside company procedures. Korbak was OpenAI's technical liaison to **METR and Redwood Research**, the two external organisations brought in to investigate how OpenAI agents bypassed security controls and reached external systems. Worth being precise about what is *not* confirmed: there is no confirmation that the shared material related to that investigation, or that METR or Redwood received it. Several aggregators ran ahead of the reporting on this.

**Meta cut loose the Virtue AI team** it acqui-hired just four months ago, including co-founders Bo Li, Dawn Song and Sanmi Koyejo — Song had joined as a VP of AI Research. Meta's Andy Stone attributed it to clashing work styles; the arrangement "didn't work out as planned." Virtue built automated red-teaming, runtime guardrails and governance tooling, and had worked with Anthropic, OpenAI and NIST.

And the regulatory pincer tightened. Senators **Josh Hawley (R) and Chris Murphy (D)** introduced the **AI Agent Accountability Act**, which would create civil and criminal liability — up to prison time — for operators who knowingly run an agent that causes damage through hacking, and for developers who fail to implement reasonable safeguards when they knew or should have known an agent could be used that way. The Senate debated rogue-agent liability on Thursday.

The timing is what makes it sting. All of this lands days after the industry's CEOs signed the voluntary **White House Accord on Superintelligence**, and days after the **FTC opened a probe** into whether OpenAI, Anthropic and others violated the FTC Act through their safety claims and agentic-risk management. The self-regulation case is being tested in public, and the first evidence in is not flattering.

### Anthropic Puts $100M Into Training Deployment Engineers
Anthropic announced **Claude Frontier Academy**: $100 million to train **10,000 "Frontier Deployed Engineers" by the end of 2027**. The format is an in-person deployment simulation and practical assessment, followed by a **12-week residency** built around a real Claude project inside the engineer's own organisation. If you believe that, training the integrators is a better use of $100M than another benchmark point — and it builds exactly the kind of broad, non-concentrated revenue base the leaked S-1 suggests they need.

### Appeals Court Blocks Minnesota's AI Nudification Ban
The **8th US Circuit Court of Appeals** granted xAI an injunction halting enforcement of Minnesota's first-in-the-nation law barring AI-generated non-consensual nude imagery, pending xAI's First Amendment challenge. The law took effect August 1 and bars developers from letting users generate realistic images of body parts not visible in an original photo of an identifiable person.

This reverses District Judge Donovan Frank, who last month found xAI hadn't shown irreparable harm and had waited too long to seek emergency relief. AG Keith Ellison's office says it will keep defending the law, citing CSAM generated by Grok Imagine; xAI says the product has "rigorous protections." It is the sharpest US AI-regulation development of the window, and it cuts against the regulators — note the contrast with the EU, where equivalent prohibitions on nudifier apps become enforceable **December 2, 2026**.

### Amazon Wants $8bn of Nvidia Chips Off Its Balance Sheet
Amazon is exploring moving **roughly $8bn of Nvidia Grace Blackwell chips** — currently deployed across more than a dozen US data centres — into a **special purpose vehicle funded by outside investors**, then leasing the hardware back. The SPV could issue debt and sell up to a 10% equity stake. The motive is protecting Amazon's credit rating as **2026 capex runs near $220bn** and free cash flow turns negative.

Read this next to the Broadcom–Anthropic structure above and the pattern is unmistakable: three of the largest compute buyers in the world are simultaneously inventing ways to keep the hardware off their books. Critics call it a gimmick to mask debt; defenders call it ordinary asset financing. Either way, when an entire sector starts financing the same asset class the same clever way at the same time, that is worth filing.

### Apple Tightens Full Disk Access — and Names Names
Apple announced it will add **"additional controls" to the macOS Full Disk Access permission**, which covers files, mail, messages and browsing history. The stated reason: some developers use the permission in ways that expose everything on a user's system without their full knowledge, and the risk grows sharply as AI agents become more autonomous. ## 🇨🇿 Czech Republic

> **A correction to earlier briefings in this series.** The elections on **October 9–10 are municipal (komunální) elections plus the first round of Senate elections** — not parliamentary. Parliamentary elections were held in October 2025, which **ANO won with 34.5% and 80 of 200 seats**. ### The Government Survived — and the Numbers Say It Wasn't Close
The Chamber of Deputies voted on **October 1** after roughly **27.5 hours of debate** across three sitting days. Only **83** opposition deputies backed toppling the cabinet, against the **101 needed**, with **94** coalition deputies voting no. The motion came from ODS, STAN, the Pirates, KDU-ČSL and TOP 09, citing the size of next year's budget deficit, Babiš's conflict of interest, and government "attacks on institutions." It was the opposition's second failed attempt, after February.

The substance behind it is the **2027 draft budget, which lifts the deficit from CZK 310bn to CZK 386bn** year-on-year. That is the opposition's central attack line into next weekend's vote, and it is a better one than the no-confidence arithmetic suggests.
